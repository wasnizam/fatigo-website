Drive

The mark on the road.

Same advisory mark in a car or a cab. Start from Now or Watch. Long hours sit heavy — pause places are estimates. Not hours-of-service. Fatigo does not tell you to pull over.

  • Car or truck. A short hop and a long haul use the same number. Watch and Lock Screen keep it with you.
  • Genius brief. Hours, miles, pause windows about every two hours, and whether the trip overlaps the usual body-clock low.
  • Pause places. Optional map stops along the way. A suggestion — you decide.
  • Fatigue on a long haul. Wrist and Lock Screen tap if the mark falls. Glance, then you choose. Still an estimate.
  • Travel log. Finished drives keep the mark you had. Export PDF or Excel for yourself — not a workplace or hours-of-service log.

Why it helps Hours in a cab raise fatigue like any long drive. The number rides with you — then you decide the next pause. Advisory only. Not HOS.

Shift

The mark through the night.

Put a work block on the calendar. Now and the next twelve hours stay in view through it. Fatigo can sketch a sleep window around a shift you already know. Not hours-of-service. Not a medical device.

  • Start or plan. Begin now, or add a later day. Coming-up and a finished shift log sit in the same pack.
  • Sleep window. An advisory window before or after the block. A short nap sketch when the gap is tight. You take it or you don’t.
  • Body clock. See if this night overlaps the usual biological-night dip — same engine as Now, on a timeline.
  • Nights occupation. Plus pack for overnight weeks. The night dip and overnight copy lean harder. Still an estimate.
  • Shift log. Tap a finished block for the alertness graph. Personal schedule, not a workplace record.

Why it helps A night is easier to judge when the block, the mark, and a sleep window sit together. You see the stretch coming — then you decide when to rest.
